Thank you for the conversation. What you described is exactly what the
Investment Map was built to do: put a live decision environment under the

You named three live decisions. Here is how I would put the instrument under
each one.

## 1. The Alabama district

We load the district parcels into their own decision environment, built on
the records of record: boundaries, ownership, land area, existing use,
assessed value. Your team assigns investment pillars parcel by parcel and
sees cost, outcomes, value created, and new tax base for each scenario, side
by side. Because your partner brings alignment rather than capital, each
scenario also carries a capital stack view: what the plan costs, what the
partner's public resources can realistically cover, and what gap remains.
That gap is the number that answers your real question, whether HCDC enters
as advisor or as equity partner, and at what level.

## 2. The Alabama five-acre site

One site, one decision, several ways in. We model the same site under each
structure your team is weighing, plus the ones I would add to the menu:

- Buy outright, cash or debt
- Option contract with an entitlement period, control before ownership
- Ground lease, the land stays with the owner
- Fee developer, fee income with no balance sheet risk
- Joint venture where the owner contributes the land as equity
- Phased takedown, buy in tranches as phases start
- Seller financing or installment terms
- Public conveyance or authority partnership if the city is at the table

Each structure becomes a scenario on the same parcel: same site, different
role, different capital, different risk, different return. Your team compares
them on one screen and documents the choice.

## 3. The Atlanta and Decatur twenty acres

This is the strongest fit of the three. We treat the site as its own corridor:
we draw candidate subdivision plans as parcels inside the twenty acres, then
test them against each other. One parcel, one use. Subdivided, mixed use.
Phased. Each concept shows total investment, outcomes, projected value, and
new annual tax base, so highest and best use stops being an opinion and
becomes a comparison. Because the seller is a bank, the acquisition scenarios
can carry distressed pricing and seller-financing assumptions alongside
market ones.

## The four capabilities you asked about

All four are extensions of the working engine, not new inventions.

1. **Construction modes.** Every parcel carries a mode: rehab, new
   construction, or demolition plus new. Rehab prices on the existing
   building square footage of record. New construction prices on proposed
   square footage. Historic preservation runs as a rehab variant that carries
   the federal and state historic credit lines in the capital stack.
2. **Sustainability option.** A toggle per scenario: a cost premium
   assumption, sustainability outcome metrics on the pillars, and the funding
   sources a green scope unlocks in the stack.
3. **Market calibration.** Alabama and Atlanta each get their own named cost
   profile: construction costs, value factors, and effective tax rates set to
   that market. Every assumption stays visible and editable, so your team can
   tune them as real bids come in.
4. **Partner exports.** Your team keeps the instrument. Partners get the
   paper: a branded brief per scenario with the recommendation, the numbers,
   and the corridor picture. No partner logins, no tool access, nothing to
   train.

Pillars remain fully editable throughout. Your team adds, renames, and
reprices them as each deal requires.
