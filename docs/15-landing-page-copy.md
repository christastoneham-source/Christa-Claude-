Many Harris County families share a home or land that was passed down without clear

## What this tool does

- Helps you understand what may be happening with your family property.
- Spots issues that may involve probate, title, taxes, or heirship.
- Tells you which records to gather and where to look.
- Points you to the right Harris County offices and trusted partners.
- Gives you clear questions to ask a licensed Texas attorney.
- Builds you a personalized **Family Property Roadmap** you can save or print.

## What this tool does NOT do

- It is **not** a lawyer and does **not** give legal advice.
- It does **not** decide who owns the property or who the heirs are.
- It does **not** tell you to file, sell, sign, or transfer anything.
- It does **not** give you a score, grade, or rating.

> "I can help you understand what issues may be present, what records may be needed,
> what public offices or partners may be involved, and what questions you may want to
> ask a licensed Texas attorney. I cannot provide legal advice, determine ownership,
> determine heirship, or tell you what legal action to take."

---

## How it works

1. **Tell us about the property.** A few simple questions — one at a time. "I don't
   know" is always an okay answer.
2. **Get your roadmap.** We explain what your answers may mean and what to do next.
3. **Take the next step.** Gather records, contact the right office, or talk to legal
   aid or an attorney — with questions already prepared.

---

## Three ways we help families

**Prevention** — If the owner is living, plan ahead so the home passes down clearly
and the family avoids future problems.

**Intervention** — If an owner has passed away or the title is unclear, get
organized: find records, understand probate and heirship questions, and prepare for
legal help.

**Preservation** — Keep the home, repair it, protect exemptions, avoid foreclosure,
and protect your family's property for the next generation.

## You're in the right place if…

- A loved one passed away and the house is still in their name.
- "The house has always been ours," but you're not sure what the paperwork says.
- You got a tax notice, a court paper, or certified mail about the property.
- Someone is pressuring you to sign or offering fast cash for the home.
- You've been told you can't get repairs or disaster help because of the title.
- You want to keep the family home — and don't know where to start.

> If you've received a court paper, a tax-sale or foreclosure notice, or someone is
> pressuring you to sign, this may need urgent attention. We'll help you organize
> your records and questions so you can reach a professional quickly.

## Your privacy matters

We only ask for what we need. **Please don't enter your Social Security number, full
bank account number, or other highly sensitive information.** We don't share your
information with any partner unless you say yes. You can stop anytime.
