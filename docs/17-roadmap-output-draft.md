# Family Property Roadmap: Harris County Heirs' Property Navigator

> about your specific situation, please speak with a licensed Texas attorney.**
>
> I can help you understand what issues may be present, what records may be needed,

## 2. What You Shared

- **Property:** A home in Kashmere Gardens, City of Houston, Harris County.
- **Occupancy:** Occupied by one family member.
- **Current owner status:** The listed owner (your father) has passed away.
- **Will:** It is not known whether there was a will.
- **Probate:** It is not known whether probate was opened.
- **Possible heirs:** There may be multiple heirs (you mentioned four siblings).
- **Taxes:** Taxes may be delinquent, and a certified letter was received from the
  tax office.
- **Exemptions:** Not confirmed.
- **Your goal:** Keep the family home and stop the tax problem.

## 3. What This May Mean

- Because a certified letter arrived from the tax office and taxes may be delinquent,
  **this may be a time-sensitive tax situation that could benefit from prompt review
  by legal aid or an attorney.** This is not a legal conclusion.
- Because the listed owner has passed away and it is not known whether there was a
  will or whether probate happened, **this may be a situation where ownership records
  need further review.** This is not a legal conclusion.
- Because several family members may be heirs, **this may be a situation where more
  than one person needs to be involved in decisions about the property.** This is not
  a legal conclusion.
- Because the family wants to keep and possibly repair the home, **title and tax
  status may affect access to assistance programs.** This is not a legal conclusion.

> **Because you mentioned a notice, court paper, tax issue, foreclosure risk, or
> pressure to sign documents, this may require urgent review by a licensed attorney
> or qualified legal aid organization. This tool cannot tell you what legal action to
> take, but it can help you organize records and questions to bring to a professional
> as soon as possible.**

## 4. Recommended Pathway or Pathways

1. **Tax Delinquency or Foreclosure Risk Pathway** *(most time-sensitive)*
2. **Deceased Owner Without Known Will Pathway**
3. **Multiple Heirs Pathway**
4. **Keep and Repair the Family Home Pathway**

## 5. Why This Pathway Was Selected

You shared that a certified letter came from the tax office and taxes may be behind,
which is why the tax pathway is listed first. You also shared that the listed owner
has passed away and the family isn't sure whether there was a will or whether probate
happened, which points to the deceased-owner and multiple-heirs pathways. Finally,
your goal is to keep and possibly repair the home, so the keep-and-repair pathway is
included.

## 6. Records to Gather

| Record | Where it may be found / who may know |
|---|---|
| Any tax notices / the certified letter | Keep the envelope and all pages; note any dates/deadlines |
| Property tax statement | Harris County Tax Assessor-Collector |
| HCAD property account | Harris Central Appraisal District (by address) |
| The deed | Harris County Clerk (real property records, by address/name) |
| Death certificate (your father's) | Texas vital records / family records |
| Will (if any) | Family members, a safe deposit box, prior attorney |
| Probate records / case number | Harris County Probate Courts / County Clerk |
| Affidavit of heirship (if any) | County Clerk records; family papers |
| Family tree & heir contact list | Build with the family (names, relationships, contacts) |
| Proof of who pays taxes/insurance/utilities | Receipts, statements |

## 7. Harris County Offices to Contact

- **Harris County Tax Assessor-Collector** — to confirm whether taxes are current or
  delinquent, ask about payment plans or deferrals, and ask whether there is a tax
  suit or sale on the account.
- **Harris Central Appraisal District (HCAD)** — to look up the property account and
  check what exemptions exist.
- **Harris County Clerk's Office** — to search real property records for the deed and
  any recorded documents.
- **Harris County Probate Courts** — to check whether a probate case was ever opened.
- **Legal aid / attorney resources** — for prompt review given the tax notice (Lone
  Star Legal Aid, Houston Volunteer Lawyers, Earl Carl Institute).

## 8. Questions to Ask an Attorney

- Is this property in tax foreclosure, and is there a deadline to respond?
- Can a payment plan, deferral, exemption, or legal defense apply to the taxes?
- What happens when someone dies without a will in Texas, and who may have an
  interest in this property?
- Is probate needed, or could an affidavit of heirship or small estate affidavit be
  appropriate?
- Can one heir act alone, or do all possible heirs need to participate?
- Can heirs apply for repair assistance if the deed is still in our father's name?
- What records are needed to clarify title before we make decisions?

## 9. Questions to Ask Public Offices

- **Tax Assessor-Collector:** "Are the taxes current or delinquent? Is there a tax
  suit or sale on this account? Is a payment plan or deferral available?"
- **HCAD:** "What is the property account for this address, and what exemptions are on
  it? Can the resident apply for a homestead exemption?"
- **County Clerk:** "How do I search real property records by address, and how do I
  get a certified copy of the deed?"
- **Probate Courts:** "Was a probate case ever opened for this person, and how do I
  find the case number?"

## 10. Non-Legal Support Referrals

- Tax exemption support (homestead / over-65 / disability / veteran review).
- Foreclosure prevention / housing counseling.
- Home repair pre-eligibility support.
- Community navigation in Kashmere Gardens / Super Neighborhood 52.

## 11. Things to Avoid Until Legal Review

- Do **not** ignore the tax notice, any court notice, or certified mail.
- Do **not** assume one sibling can act for everyone without legal documents.
- Do **not** sign any deed, sale contract, waiver, or quitclaim under pressure.
- Do **not** accept a fast cash offer without reviewing title, taxes, and family
  authority.
- Do **not** spend major money on repairs before understanding ownership and tax
  status.
- Do **not** rely only on family memory if public records say something different.

## 12. Suggested Next 30 Days

1. Put the certified letter and any notices in a folder; write down any deadlines.
2. Call or visit the Tax Assessor-Collector to confirm the tax status and ask about
   payment options.
3. Look up the HCAD property account online by address.
4. Search for the deed in Harris County real property records (or ask the Clerk for
   help).
5. Ask family members whether there was a will, a trust, or any prior court action.
6. Start a simple family tree and heir contact list.
7. Contact legal aid or a licensed Texas attorney promptly because of the tax notice.

## 13. Longer-Term Protection Steps

- Once title is clearer, review estate planning so the home passes down cleanly going
  forward.
- Review property tax exemptions every year.
- Keep all property documents organized in one place.
- Hold a family meeting to align on the goal for the property.
- Get legal review before signing anything related to the property.
- Plan for home repair and preservation to keep the home safe and protect your
  family's generational wealth.

## 14. Closing Message

> You do not have to resolve every issue at once. The first step is understanding
> what is known, what is missing, and who can help. This roadmap is designed to help
> you prepare for informed conversations with qualified professionals and public
> agencies.
