# Deliverable 19 — Funding Strategy & The Ask

> **Note:** The dollar figures below are **illustrative planning estimates** to help
> you structure a request — not financial, legal, or grant-writing advice. Validate
> every number against your organization's real costs and the specific funder's
> grant guidelines and maximums before submitting.

## Why a bank funds this (lead with their motivation, not yours)

Banking institutions fund community work like this through **CRA (Community
Reinvestment Act)** obligations, foundation/philanthropy arms, and financial-
inclusion or housing-stability initiatives. This project hits the exact themes
reviewers reward:

- **Generational wealth & financial inclusion** — heirs' property is a leading,
  often-invisible driver of Black and brown wealth loss; clearing tangled title
  *preserves* household wealth.
- **Displacement & foreclosure prevention** — keeps families housed and properties
  on the tax roll.
- **Place-based equity** — Kashmere Gardens / Super Neighborhood 52 is a historically
  underinvested, often-redlined community; targeted investment is a strong CRA story.
- **Leverage & replicability** — a small grant produces a *model* other neighborhoods
  (and the bank's other markets) can reuse.
- **Trusted intermediary** — Houston Land Bank is a credible, mission-aligned steward
  with existing community relationships.

**One-line pitch:** *"Help us turn a working demo into a community-validated
navigator that keeps Harris County families in their homes and protects generational
wealth — starting in Kashmere Gardens, built to replicate."*

## What to ask for (frame it as a planning/pilot-readiness grant)

Do **not** ask for "software." Ask for an **operational + community-engagement
grant** that funds Phase 1: listening sessions, stakeholder testing, expert content
review, and development of a full pilot proposal. This is smaller, lower-risk for the
funder, and sets up a larger Phase 2 ask once you have evidence.

## How much — a tiered ask

Present a **recommended** number with a lean fallback and a stretch. Most bank
community/CRA grants for a planning phase land in the **$50k–$150k** range; pilots run
**$150k–$350k+**.

### Tier 1 — Lean ("pilot-readiness minimum") ≈ **$75,000**, ~6 months
Just enough to validate and produce a fundable proposal.

| Line item | Est. |
|---|---|
| Project lead / navigator coordinator (part-time) | $22,000 |
| Community listening sessions + participant stipends | $14,000 |
| Content verification + legal review (Texas attorneys / legal aid) | $12,000 |
| Spanish translation + plain-language/accessibility review | $9,000 |
| Demo refinement (content, fixes, branding) | $8,000 |
| Evaluation framework design | $4,000 |
| Outreach, materials, community touchpoints | $3,000 |
| Admin / indirect (~4%) | $3,000 |
| **Total** | **$75,000** |

### Tier 2 — Recommended ("design + pilot-readiness") ≈ **$150,000**, ~9 months
Fuller community engagement, partner convening, and a working prototype of the
partner referral system.

| Line item | Est. |
|---|---|
| Project lead / navigator coordinator | $40,000 |
| 4–6 listening sessions + usability testing + stipends | $25,000 |
| Partner convening + MOUs (legal aid, tax, probate, repair, housing) | $12,000 |
| Content verification + full legal review | $18,000 |
| Full education card library + plain-language editing | $10,000 |
| Spanish + accessibility (ADA/WCAG) audit & fixes | $12,000 |
| Partner referral / warm-handoff prototype (Stage 3 start) | $15,000 |
| Evaluation framework + reporting dashboard design | $8,000 |
| Outreach, translation, materials, trusted-messenger network | $7,000 |
| Admin / indirect (~2%) | $3,000 |
| **Total** | **$150,000** |

### Tier 3 — Stretch ("design + early pilot") ≈ **$250,000–$300,000**, ~12 months
Everything in Tier 2 plus a **staffed live pilot** serving real families with partner
referrals and measured outcomes (a bilingual navigator, expanded stipends, and a
completed referral system).

> **How to present it:** ask for **Tier 2 (~$150k)** as the primary request, name
> Tier 1 as a "phase-able minimum," and mention Tier 3 as the natural next step once
> Phase 1 proves demand. This signals scope discipline and gives the funder room to
> say yes at a level they're comfortable with.

## What the grant produces (deliverables the funder can hold you to)

- A **community-validated, legally reviewed** navigator (English + Spanish).
- A report from **4–6 listening sessions** and usability testing.
- Signed or in-progress **partner MOUs**.
- An **evaluation framework** + baseline metrics.
- A **full Phase 2 pilot proposal** (the funder's natural next investment).

## Metrics funders care about (no individual scores — aggregate only)

- Families reached / screeners completed.
- Pathway distribution; documents-missing patterns.
- Referrals made and **closed-loop completion rate**.
- Foreclosure/tax-sale escalations surfaced and routed.
- Time from first contact to referral.
- Denial reasons and bottlenecks (used to improve the model).
- Reach in Kashmere Gardens / SN 52; language needs served.

## The ask packet (what to send)

1. **One-page cover letter / executive summary** (the one-line pitch + the ask).
2. **The live demo link** (Netlify) — let them touch it.
3. **2-page program brief**: problem, solution, who you serve, the roadmap
   ([Deliverable 18](./18-improvement-roadmap.md)).
4. **Budget** (the tier you're requesting, with line items).
5. **Outcomes & evaluation** summary.
6. **Org credibility**: Houston Land Bank's mission, track record, partners.
7. **Sustainability**: how Phase 1 leads to a fundable pilot and replication.

## Strategic sequencing

1. **Now:** stand up the live demo; finalize the brief + budget.
2. **Outreach:** approach the bank's CRA officer / foundation program staff first
   (warm intro if possible) before formal submission — understand their cycle,
   priorities, and cap.
3. **Submit** Tier 2 as a planning/pilot-readiness grant.
4. **Deliver** Phase 1; use the evidence to submit the larger Phase 2 pilot ask
   (to the same funder and/or additional funders, reducing single-funder risk).

## How AI fits the ask (a likely funder question)

Expect funders to ask "where's the AI?" Answer in two parts:

- **Build-time AI (now):** AI assistance builds and iterates the tool, which is how a
  small team ships a credible bilingual demo without an external software consultant.
- **Runtime AI (Phase 2):** a guarded, plain-language conversational helper for
  families is a **funded Phase 2 feature** — it needs a backend, safety layer,
  attorney review, and monitoring, so it is staged deliberately rather than rushed
  into a legal-adjacent demo.

This framing is a strength: it shows you use AI where it helps *and* that you
understand the safety work required before AI talks to families. The Phase 1 demo
stays a rules-based, attorney-reviewable engine, hosted as a free static file with no
dependency on a third-party app-builder account.

## A candid note on doing this without an external consultant

You can absolutely lead Phase 1 in-house. The places to *buy expertise as scoped line
items* (not a general consultant retainer) are: **legal review** (non-negotiable for
liability), **evaluation design**, and **professional translation**. Everything else
— strategy, content, the demo, community engagement — you and your team already have
in hand in this repository.
