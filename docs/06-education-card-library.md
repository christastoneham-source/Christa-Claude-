# Deliverable 6 — Education Card Library Outline

Every card is short, plain-language, and uses the **same five-part structure**.
Cards are surfaced inline in the screener ("What's a deed?") and browsable at
`/education`. None of these cards give legal advice or reach legal conclusions.

## Card template

```
TITLE
1. What it is        — plain-language definition (2–4 sentences)
2. Why it matters    — why a family dealing with heirs' property should care
3. Records involved  — documents that relate to this topic
4. Harris County office — which office/partner may be relevant
5. Ask an attorney/agency — one or two questions to bring to a professional
```

Every card also carries the standing footer: *"This is general education, not legal
advice. A licensed Texas attorney can advise on your specific situation."*

## Fully drafted example cards

### Heirs' property
1. **What it is** — A home or land passed down informally when an owner dies, so
   the property ends up shared among several relatives without clear, updated
   paperwork showing who owns what.
2. **Why it matters** — Shared, undocumented ownership can make it hard to repair,
   sell, refinance, insure, or get assistance, and can put the family home at risk.
3. **Records involved** — Deed, death certificate(s), will (if any), probate
   records, affidavit of heirship, tax records, family tree.
4. **Office** — Harris County Clerk (real property & probate records); HCAD
   (property account); legal aid for review.
5. **Ask an attorney** — "Is this property heirs' property, and what records are
   needed to clarify who has an interest?"

### Tangled title
1. **What it is** — When the public record does not clearly show who currently owns
   a property — often because a deceased owner's name is still on the deed.
2. **Why it matters** — A tangled title can block repairs, sales, loans,
   exemptions, and disaster or repair assistance.
3. **Records involved** — Deed, probate records, court orders, affidavits, tax
   records.
4. **Office** — Harris County Clerk; Probate Courts; legal aid.
5. **Ask an attorney** — "What steps could clear up the title on this property?"

### Deed
1. **What it is** — A legal document that transfers ownership of real property and
   is recorded in the county real property records.
2. **Why it matters** — The deed shows whose name is on the property in the public
   record, which may differ from who the family believes owns it.
3. **Records involved** — The deed itself, prior deeds, deeds of trust, releases.
4. **Office** — Harris County Clerk (recording & searching real property records).
5. **Ask an attorney** — "Whose name is on the current recorded deed, and does it
   match what my family believes?"

### Probate
1. **What it is** — The court-supervised process for handling a deceased person's
   estate — proving a will (if any) and authorizing someone to settle the estate.
2. **Why it matters** — Whether probate happened, and how, may affect who can act
   for the estate and how property can be transferred.
3. **Records involved** — Will, death certificate, probate application, court
   orders, executor/administrator paperwork, probate case number.
4. **Office** — Harris County Probate Courts; Harris County Clerk (probate records).
5. **Ask an attorney** — "Does this estate need to be probated, and who would have
   authority to act?"

### Affidavit of heirship
1. **What it is** — A sworn statement, often by disinterested witnesses, describing
   a deceased person's family history and possible heirs; sometimes used to address
   ownership without full probate.
2. **Why it matters** — It may be one option families explore, but whether it is
   appropriate depends on the situation and requires legal review.
3. **Records involved** — The affidavit, family tree, death certificate, deed.
4. **Office** — Harris County Clerk (recording); legal aid/attorney to prepare.
5. **Ask an attorney** — "Is an affidavit of heirship appropriate here, or is
   another process needed?"

### Homestead exemption
1. **What it is** — A property tax break for a person's primary residence that can
   lower the taxable value of the home.
2. **Why it matters** — It may reduce property taxes, but it does not by itself
   resolve who owns the property or clear title.
3. **Records involved** — Exemption application, Texas ID/driver's license matching
   the property address, HCAD account.
4. **Office** — HCAD (exemptions); Tax Assessor-Collector (tax bills).
5. **Ask an attorney/agency** — "Can the person living here qualify for a homestead
   exemption while the title is still being clarified?"

### Tax foreclosure / tax sale
1. **What it is** — A legal process where unpaid property taxes can lead to a
   lawsuit and, eventually, a sale of the property.
2. **Why it matters** — Deadlines are involved; this is one of the most urgent
   situations a family can face and may require prompt legal help.
3. **Records involved** — Tax statements, lawsuit/court papers, notices, payment
   plan or deferral records.
4. **Office** — Tax Assessor-Collector (payment options); legal aid/attorney
   urgently if a lawsuit or sale notice exists.
5. **Ask an attorney** — "Is this property in tax foreclosure, is there a deadline
   to respond, and what options exist?"  *(This is an escalation topic — see
   [Deliverable 11](./11-escalation-flag-logic.md).)*

### Transfer on death deed
1. **What it is** — A document a living owner can record that names who receives the
   property automatically at the owner's death, without probate.
2. **Why it matters** — For living owners, it is one prevention tool that may avoid
   future heirs' property problems; suitability requires legal review.
3. **Records involved** — The recorded transfer on death deed, current deed.
4. **Office** — Harris County Clerk (recording); attorney to prepare.
5. **Ask an attorney** — "Would a transfer on death deed be appropriate for this
   property and family?"

### Anti-predatory warning signs
1. **What it is** — Red flags that someone may be trying to take advantage of a
   family with unclear title — fast cash offers, pressure to sign quickly, vague
   promises, or discouraging legal review.
2. **Why it matters** — Families with tangled title are frequently targeted;
   signing under pressure can cause irreversible loss.
3. **Records involved** — Any contract, deed, waiver, or quitclaim presented for
   signature; keep copies, sign nothing under pressure.
4. **Office** — Legal aid; attorney review before signing anything.
5. **Ask an attorney** — "Someone is pressuring me to sign — what should I review
   before doing anything?"  *(Escalation topic.)*

### When to talk to an attorney
1. **What it is** — Guidance on situations where professional legal help is
   especially important.
2. **Why it matters** — Court papers, tax-sale/foreclosure notices, family
   disputes, pressure to sign, and unclear ownership all benefit from legal review.
3. **Records involved** — Whatever records you already have, gathered in one folder.
4. **Office** — Lone Star Legal Aid, Houston Volunteer Lawyers, Earl Carl Institute,
   Houston Bar Association resources.
5. **Ask an attorney** — "Here is my situation and the records I have — what are my
   options and next steps?"
