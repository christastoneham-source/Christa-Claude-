## Paste-ready prompt

Design a one-page PDF (US Letter, portrait) for The Investment Map, the
flagship engagement of Connecting Dots and Strategies (Christa Stoneham). It is
a sales one-pager for foundations and community investment organizations
funding place-based change. Elegant, editorial, confident - an architect's
document, not a tech flyer.

BRAND SYSTEM (non-negotiable):
- Background Cream #FCFAF1. Text Ink #101C28.
- Headings and the title in Fraunces SemiBold; body, labels, and UI text in Inter.
- Four brand colors, always in this fixed order: Brick #D95448, Gold #F1BA4B,
  Grove #47AB4F, Turquoise #00BCC5. Turquoise is the only action/link color.
- Place the four-dot mark (one small dot of each color, in that order, never
  reordered) beside the logotype. Include one thin spectrum rule running Brick
  on the left to Turquoise on the right, through Gold and Grove - never flipped.
- No em dashes anywhere. Use commas, periods, or hyphens with spaces.
- Tagline: "Reclaiming the map." appears once, near the title or the footer.

PAGE ARCHITECTURE (top to bottom):

1. Header: four-dot mark + "Connecting Dots and Strategies." Title: "The
   Investment Map." Subtitle: "A decision engagement for organizations funding
   place-based change. Two decades of practice, delivered at AI speed." Kicker
   line in small caps: "STAKEHOLDER-ALIGNED. FUNDER-READY. ACCOUNTABLE."
   Spectrum rule beneath.

2. The Problem (short block): "You hold the budget and the will to move. What
   you lack is a defensible path. Priorities compete, stakeholders pull apart,
   data lives in ten places, and the tradeoffs stay unclear. Plans sit while
   neighborhoods wait, and the decision you finally make is slow, scattered,
   and hard to defend."

3. Why It Works (three columns): Speed - scenarios built in days, not
   quarters. Judgment - two decades of deals, capital, and community practice
   shaped the system's design. Voice - community priorities sit inside the
   decision, not after the fact.

4. The 90 Day Arc (three steps on a horizontal band, each keyed to a brand
   color): 1-30 Discovery - goals, stakeholders, opportunities, and risks,
   mapped. 31-60 Scenarios - your team weighs options side by side and
   documents the choice. 61-90 Roadmap - alignment secured, implementation
   pathway locked.

5. What Is Included (five numbered items, compact):
   01 Community Investment Discovery - working sessions plus a written
      assessment.
   02 Decision Environment - a live scenario instrument built on your
      corridor's real records: every parcel with ownership, zoning, assessed
      value, and buildings of record; investment options compared side by side
      on outcomes, cost per outcome, and the property value and new annual tax
      base each scenario creates; a hosted link your team keeps, with
      board-ready exports.
   03 Stakeholder Map and Engagement Sequence - decision makers, community
      voices, partners, and blockers, named and ordered.
   04 Implementation Roadmap - phases, partner roles, funding sequence, and
      execution steps for the year after the decision.
   05 Strategic Advisory - working sessions and office hours across the
      engagement.

6. The Outcomes (three checkmarked lines): A decision your board and funders
   stand behind - where to invest, at what level, in what order. One shared
   picture - staff, board, funders, and community on the same map and the same
   numbers. A roadmap ready to execute the day the engagement ends.
   Tag: "Fixed scope. 90 days."

7. Who Is Behind the Work: [use the existing bio paragraph verbatim] with the
   stat band: [CONFIRM FINAL NUMBERS - must match the deck: $150M+ community
   investment led / property value regenerated / capital raised / projects
   delivered / residents reached / affordable homes].

8. Footer: Turquoise CTA "Start the conversation." · info@christastoneham.com
   · CHRISTASTONEHAM.COM · "Connecting Dots and Strategies · Reclaiming the map."

DO NOT: use stock imagery, tech-startup gradients, screenshots of the tool,
the word "software" as the product identity, or any claim of environmental,
legal, or appraisal determinations. The instrument is proof of the practice;
the practice is what is being bought.
