# Affordable Housing Finance — Plain-Language Notes for Reviewers

Distilled from the HR&A *Affordable Housing Finance 101* training (May 19, 2026)
so reviewers can read the financial parts of a proposal with confidence.

## Why affordable projects need extra funding
Development costs (land, hard costs, soft costs, profit) determine how much money
must be raised. The building then has to pay its bills from rent. When rents are
held **affordable**, they bring in less income — so the project usually **can't
support enough debt to cover all its costs.** That shortfall is the **funding
gap**, and it's normally filled with extra sources: **LIHTC equity**, local gap
financing, soft/subordinate debt, and grants. *Deeper affordability = bigger gap
= more outside sources needed.*

## The two sides of every project
- **Sources and uses** = where the money comes from (sources) and what it pays
  for (uses). The two must balance.
- **Capital stack** = the layers of money, by who gets paid back first:
  - **Senior debt** — main loan; lowest risk, paid first.
  - **Subordinate debt** ("must-pay" or "soft-pay") — paid after senior.
  - **Equity** — owner/investor money at risk, paid last, expects highest return.
  - In affordable deals, **LIHTC equity** and **supportive-service dollars** are
    added layers that make deeper affordability possible.

## How a lender sizes the mortgage
1. **Rent − Operating Expenses = NOI** (net operating income).
2. The loan is sized so NOI comfortably covers the loan payment.
3. **DSCR (debt service coverage ratio) = NOI ÷ debt payment.** A 1.20 DSCR
   means ~83% of NOI goes to debt and ~17% is a cushion for downside risk.
   More uncertainty → lenders want a higher DSCR → smaller loan.
4. Loan size also depends on **interest rate** and **amortization** (longer
   amortization and lower rates support a bigger loan).

**Reviewer takeaways:**
- If a proposal promises deep affordability (e.g., 50% AMI) but the pro forma
  rents look closer to 80% AMI, the numbers may not work without another subsidy
  — ask about it.
- If LIHTC equity is listed, ask **which path** (4% credits with tax-exempt
  bonds vs. 9% competitive credits) — each has different timing, risk, and
  competitiveness — and whether it's **committed, estimated, or speculative.**
- A credible deal shows a realistic DSCR, identified sources with status
  (hard/soft/pending), and a clear plan to close any remaining gap.

## Who's who in the ecosystem
- **Government** (federal/state/city/PHAs): LIHTC allocation, tax-exempt bonds,
  soft subsidies (HOME/CDBG), housing trust funds.
- **Nonprofits/CDFIs:** gap financing, technical assistance, services.
- **Developers/owners:** plan, build, own, operate; serve as guarantors.
- **Private/for-profit:** banks (senior debt), syndicators (sell LIHTC to
  investors), equity investors.

For 7811 Harrisburg specifically: **HLB provides no financing guarantees** and
retains fee simple; the **Development Partner is the guarantor**, and under a
ground lease HLB shares in value via capitalized payments + a negotiated % of NOI.
